# Track-shape reconstruction from phone recordings

## 8 November update

Replaying all three preserved exports now gives **46 distinct journeys, 23 per
branch**, including four new November recordings. The gyro-curvature shape
comparison gets 24/46 (52%) correct when each journey is omitted from its own
reference; direct acceleration integration gets 27/46 (59%). Neither supports
replacing the live branch predictor. These are whole-journey retrospective
comparisons, not live predictions.

The inferred path-length medians remain implausibly short: 688 m left and 725 m
right for gyro plus inferred speed. Thirty-four journeys look stationary in the
opening second, which remains an assumption rather than a measured initial
speed. The experimental shape was therefore kept out of the live map.

The tools now share the main content-deduplicating loader and reject contradictory
labels for the same raw journey. Partially missing gravity is excluded rather
than silently treated as a measured zero. Latest individual traces and detailed

study below is retained as historical context.

## Result

The recordings support a **repeatable relative bend profile**, but they do not
yet support a physically scaled or geographically located track reconstruction.
The figure generated by `tools/reconstruct-track-shape.mjs` shows every
recording faintly and the pointwise branch medians prominently. Its horizontal
axis is normalized inferred distance and its vertical axis is lateral movement
as a share of that trip's inferred length. It is not a map and it does not place
the junction.

The plotted traces were generated locally in the ignored `test-results/`
directory. They contain individual journey-derived lines; do not publish those
lines or the accompanying per-trip JSON. The tool reads the preserved raw
exports but does not modify them.

## What the recordings contain

The two preserved exports contain 42 unique journeys: 21 labelled left and 21
labelled right. The October export includes the earlier 35 and adds 7; it does
not contain 42 new journeys. Samples are about 60 Hz and include linear
acceleration, three-axis rotation rate, and a gravity estimate. The first
second has low acceleration RMS in 30 of 42 trips, but that alone cannot prove
that the phone was still: starting the recording can move it, and the train may
begin moving immediately. A candidate low-motion gyro-calibration window was
found in 31 of 42 trips; the other 11 use no gyro offset correction.

The app's current position marker still means progress through a historical
motion profile. It is not metres. The reconstruction experiment below was not
promoted into live position tracking because the scale and branch geometry did
not pass the physical checks.

## Methods compared

1. **Existing movement-profile matcher.** This is the deployed, live-safe
   reference: stationary observations do not advance position, and the two
   route hypotheses remain separate. Leave-one-journey-out error against the
   *detected moving-time fraction* was 4.25 percentage points, versus 4.36 for
   a simple moving-time clock and 23.38 for unconstrained nearest-signature
   matching. That is only a timing proxy, not physical position validation.
2. **Direct inertial path.** Gravity-aligned acceleration was integrated to
   velocity and position, using zero-speed assumptions at the start/end and
   long low-motion intervals. Per-segment acceleration offsets were fitted so
   speed returned to zero at each proposed stop.
3. **Gyro curvature path.** The same acceleration estimate supplies speed; the
   gravity-axis gyro supplies turning rate. The track relation
   `curvature = yaw rate / speed` is used only while moving, and the resulting
   heading is integrated over inferred distance. This avoids using lateral
   acceleration itself as the main source of curve direction.

For the two candidate paths, each journey is rotated to its own estimated
start-to-end direction and scaled to its own inferred length before taking a
pointwise median. This removes unknown phone heading and some speed variation,
but it also discards absolute orientation and distance. The transformation is
only suitable for a relative shape comparison.

## Historical-data checks

| Check | Result | What it means |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Unique journeys in preserved exports | 42 (21 per branch) | Fewer than the 50+ mentioned; other device-only recordings are not present in this folder. |
| Gyro-curvature inferred path length | left 628 m median; right 725 m median | Candidate estimate, not a distance measurement. |
| Straight-line distance between TfL Stockwell and Brixton station points | 1,169 m | Coarse external sanity check; route length must be at least this long if the station points represent the endpoints. |
| Median acceleration correction within proposed moving sections | left 0.58 m/s²; right 0.52 m/s² | Far too large to treat the candidate speed scale as established. |
| Gyro-curvature shape-only leave-one-journey-out branch match | 24/42 (57%) | Weak; not enough to identify the branch reliably. |
| Direct-path shape-only leave-one-journey-out branch match | 26/42 (62%) | Better, but still inconclusive (one-sided binomial p≈0.082 before accounting for method selection). |
| Median within-branch trace deviation | 3.4% and 3.7% of inferred length | Repeatability after each trip has been rotated and rescaled; not geographic accuracy. |

Both path methods produce broad individual scatter. Their branch medians are
close, and full-trip gyro heading changes substantially depending on how gyro
bias is estimated: the no-offset earlier analysis gave left/right medians of
about 32°/32°, while the current candidate calibration gives about 35°/25°.
No point on the plot can be identified as the junction from these labels alone.
The low-motion detector can mistake smooth travel for a stop; forcing zero
speed there changes the inferred scale. A single station start at rest does not
solve this ambiguity.

As a separate implementation test, a synthetic curved trip with two starts,
stops, and an added 30-second dwell recovered its known 300 m route length
within 5 m; adding the dwell changed the estimate by less than 3 m. This checks
the integration and pause bookkeeping under known clean inputs, not the
accuracy of the phone recordings.

## Recommended use

- Keep the live app's relative-progress tracker as the operational position
  estimate. Do not feed this experimental curve into prediction confidence or
  announce a junction crossing from it.
- Use the faint-trace/median figure as a research diagnostic. It is useful for
  spotting shared broad movement signatures and obvious outliers, but must
  retain its uncertainty label.
- To establish actual scale and branch shape, collect at least one independent
  reference: a rigidly mounted calibrated logger with odometer/GNSS outside the
  tunnel, exact tunnel chainage, or a suitably accurate surveyed track map.
  A repeatable visual mean alone cannot distinguish a shared sensor bias from
  real railway geometry.

## High-precision external maps

I checked public TfL data, planning documents, engineering publications, map
datasets, and archive catalogues for an as-built tunnel centerline or a
surveyed drawing of the Brixton crossover. I found no public downloadable
survey or coordinate table for this section. TfL confirms that it holds
detailed route GIS and thousands of track-geometry runs, but the relevant
detailed data has not been released in the FOI responses checked. Station
points and OpenStreetMap are useful coarse references, not a faithful tunnel
survey; the OSM Victoria Line mapping notes warn that mapped shaft references
can be several metres off.

There is one useful engineering anchor: a published account of the 2000
Brixton crossover replacement places it about 60 m north of Brixton, about
25 m below ground, and about 120 m long. It says the engineers checked the
alignment using a control-network survey and jigs, but publishes no scaled
plan or measured coordinates. Those facts can support an approximate relative
landmark, not the crossover's exact curves, branch spacing, or chainage.
Accordingly, the figure remains an inertial, normalized comparison and does
not draw an externally invented map. The full source audit and archive leads
are in `research_notes/Inertial Track Shape Reconstruction/high_precision_maps.md`.

## Rebuild and test

Run from the project root, passing the private exports explicitly:

```text
npm run analyze:track-shape -- victoria-training-2026-09-27-19L16R.json victoria-training-2026-10-30-21L21R.json
node --test tests/track-shape-reconstruction.test.mjs
```

The script deduplicates complete raw arrays by SHA-256 and writes an SVG plus
compact per-trip traces to ignored `test-results/`. It never writes back to the
source exports. Synthetic tests check normalized endpoints, known distance,
and that an inserted 30-second dwell does not advance estimated distance.

## Sources

- Apple, [Core Motion device-motion sensor fusion](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmdevicemotion): gravity, attitude, and rotation-rate concepts. The browser events in these recordings are not a direct native Core Motion logger.
- Zhou et al., [railway curvature from gyro and speed](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-91255-x): spatial curvature is derived from yaw rate and running speed, with stationary gyro calibration and smoothing.
- Heirich et al., [Bayesian train localization with IMU and track map](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2016/2672640): acceleration bias and map constraints are modelled explicitly; acceleration integration alone drifts.
- Vinkó et al., [smartphone sensors for railway track geometry](https://doi.org/10.3311/PPci.20187): successful geometry measurements used a rigidly mounted phone and independent reference equipment.
- TfL, [Victoria Line route sequence](https://api.tfl.gov.uk/Line/victoria/Route/Sequence/inbound?serviceTypes=Regular&excludeCrowding=true): station-level route context, not surveyed tunnel or crossover geometry.
- TfL, [FOI response about track-geometry records](https://tfl.gov.uk/corporate/transparency/freedom-of-information/foi-request-detail?referenceId=FOI-2559-2627): confirms that detailed track geometry is held, but the requested extract was not released through that request.
- TfL, [FOI response about detailed Underground route GIS](https://tfl.gov.uk/corporate/transparency/freedom-of-information/foi-request-detail?referenceId=FOI-4057-2324): detailed GIS was withheld; a public overview map was offered instead.
- Ingenia, [“Crossing the rails at Brixton”](https://ingenia.org.uk/articles/crossing-the-rails-at-brixton-replacing-the-uks-busiest-rail-junction): approximate crossover location, depth, length, and description of survey checks, without the survey drawing.
- OpenStreetMap Wiki, [Victoria Line mapping note](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Victoria_Line): documents how the public tunnel route was approximated and its positional limitations.
